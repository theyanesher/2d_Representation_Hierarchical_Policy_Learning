Positional Embedding Strategies for Heatmap-Conditioned Visual Encoders
========================================================================

Context
-------
We have a 4-channel ghost heatmap where each channel i encodes the distance
from every pixel to gripper keypoint i:

    channel_i[px, py] = ( ||pixel - keypoint_i|| / max_dist / sigma )^0.5 * 255

The ^0.5 transformation gives steep gradients near the keypoint and flat
gradients far away — high resolution near the gripper, low resolution far away.

Each patch in the image therefore has a 4D distance vector:
    pos_patch = [d0, d1, d2, d3]

where d_i is the distance from that patch to keypoint i. This is a valid
coordinate system — given 4 non-collinear reference points, every 2D pixel
position is uniquely determined by its distances to them (trilateration / GPS).


Strategy 1: Additive Positional Embedding (Current Implementation)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
In ViTHeatmapPosEmbedding and DINOHoMMIStyleEmbedding:

    token_i = content_i + heatmap_pos_embed_i

where heatmap_pos_embed_i is produced by Conv2d on the 4-channel distance field.

Problem: content and position are entangled after this addition.
When computing attention:

    q_i . k_j = (W_Q * token_i) . (W_K * token_j)
              = (W_Q * (content_i + pos_i)) . (W_K * (content_j + pos_j))

This expands to 4 terms:

    = W_Q*content_i . W_K*content_j     <- content-content (desired)
    + W_Q*pos_i     . W_K*pos_j         <- pos-pos (implicit attention bias)
    + W_Q*content_i . W_K*pos_j         <- unwanted cross term
    + W_Q*pos_i     . W_K*content_j     <- unwanted cross term

The cross terms are noise. The pos-pos term is effectively an attention bias
that the network cannot remove. However, the network CAN partially disentangle
content from position through subsequent transformer layers — so this mixing
is not permanent.

Advantage: Simple, already implemented, works in practice.
Disadvantage: Content and position mixed; cross terms pollute attention scores.


Strategy 2: Attention Bias
--------------------------
Add a heatmap-derived scalar directly to the attention logits:

    Attention(i,j) = softmax( q_i.k_j / sqrt(d)  +  bias(h_i, h_j) )

where bias(h_i, h_j) = MLP([h_i || h_j]) -> scalar.

This is worse than Strategy 1 in a subtle but important way:
- In Strategy 1, the mixing happens at the token level and subsequent layers
  can partially correct it.
- Here the bias is injected directly into attention weights AFTER the dot
  product. The network has no mechanism to disentangle or correct it —
  the positional bias is permanently baked into which tokens attend to which,
  at every layer it is applied.

The W_Q*pos_i . W_K*pos_j term from Strategy 1 IS an implicit attention bias.
Strategy 2 just makes this explicit and adds it directly — skipping even the
partial correction opportunity that Strategy 1 allows.

Advantage: Directly modulates attention, interpretable.
Disadvantage: Hardcoded into attention weights, network cannot disentangle it,
              strictly worse than Strategy 1 in terms of correctability.


Strategy 3: RoPE with Heatmap Coordinates (Cleanest)
-----------------------------------------------------
Use the heatmap distance values [d0, d1, d2, d3] as rotation angles in a
RoPE-style mechanism, applied to Q and K AFTER the linear projection:

    q_i = R(pos_i) . W_Q . content_i
    k_j = R(pos_j) . W_K . content_j

where R(pos) is a block-diagonal rotation matrix with one 2D rotation block
per distance channel:

    R(pos_i) = block_diag( R(d0_i * theta_0),
                            R(d1_i * theta_1),
                            R(d2_i * theta_2),
                            R(d3_i * theta_3) )

The dot product then becomes:

    q_i . k_j = (W_Q * content_i)^T . R(pos_i)^T . R(pos_j) . (W_K * content_j)
              = (W_Q * content_i)^T . R(pos_j - pos_i) . (W_K * content_j)

Key properties:
- Position enters MULTIPLICATIVELY as R(pos_j - pos_i) — a rotation between
  the two content vectors.
- There is NO additive bias term.
- There are NO content-position cross terms.
- Content and position are fully separated.
- Attention between patch i and patch j is modulated by how differently they
  are positioned relative to each of the 4 gripper keypoints.

Two patches that are similarly positioned relative to all 4 keypoints
(R(pos_j - pos_i) ≈ identity) attend strongly to each other. Two patches
with very different gripper-relative positions attend less.

Advantage: No entanglement, no bias, mathematically clean.
Disadvantage: Requires implementing custom RoPE with heatmap coordinates;
              loses absolute spatial grid structure (patches with same
              [d0,d1,d2,d3] but different image locations are indistinguishable).


Comparison Table
----------------
Strategy               | Entanglement | Network can correct? | Bias injected?
-----------------------|--------------|---------------------|---------------
Additive embed (cur.)  | Yes          | Partially (via depth)| Implicitly
Attention bias         | Yes          | No                  | Explicitly
RoPE w/ heatmap coords | No           | N/A (never mixed)   | No


Single Convolution on Heatmap == Implicit Trilateration Coordinate System
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
The key insight connecting Strategy 1 (current) and Strategy 3 (RoPE):

When use_single_conv=False (simple Conv2d, patch_size stride):
    patch at (px, py) -> Conv2d -> embed vector f([d0, d1, d2, d3])

When use_single_conv=True (ResNet backbone + AdaptiveAvgPool2d):
    patch at (px, py) -> deep conv -> richer f([d0, d1, d2, d3] + context)

In both cases, the conv output is a FUNCTION of the 4 distance values at
that patch. Two patches with similar [d0, d1, d2, d3] produce similar conv
output vectors. When these are added to RGB tokens and then dot-producted:

    q_i . k_j is large  <=>  patches i,j have similar heatmap embeddings
                         <=>  patches i,j are similarly positioned relative
                              to all 4 gripper keypoints

This is EXACTLY what the RoPE trilateration coordinate system encodes —
patches that are near the same gripper region (same distance vector) attend
more to each other. The difference is:

- Conv approach: implicit, learned, soft — the network decides how much
  weight to put on the heatmap signal vs content
- RoPE approach: explicit, geometric, principled — the distance values
  directly set rotation angles with no learning involved

The ^0.5 nonlinearity in the distance field gives the conv high resolution
near keypoints (where gradient is steep) and low resolution far away —
a natural inductive bias that the conv filters can exploit. This is the
"Idea 3" discussed during implementation of use_single_conv=True.

So the current implementation with use_single_conv=True is effectively doing
soft implicit trilateration as a positional signal, just without the clean
mathematical separation that RoPE would provide.


Conclusion
----------
1. Current additive approach (use_single_conv=True) already implicitly
   captures the trilateration structure of the 4-channel distance field.

2. Attention bias is strictly worse than additive embedding — no advantage,
   loses the ability to disentangle.

3. RoPE with heatmap coordinates is the mathematically cleanest approach —
   no entanglement, no implicit bias — but requires custom implementation
   and loses absolute spatial grid structure.

4. The practical gap between the current implementation and RoPE may be
   small if the transformer is deep enough to disentangle content from
   the additive positional signal across layers.
